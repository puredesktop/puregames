# PureGames

Free, open-source games for [PureDesktop](https://puredesktop.ai), published under the [MIT licence](LICENSE). Seven original games share one native app: **Vector Wake**, a solo or 1–4-player cooperative 3D wireframe cockpit shooter; **Letterlight**, a solo word-discovery puzzle; **Tether Crew**, a cooperative physics salvage game; **Word Loom**, a cooperative word puzzle; **Star Skirmish**, a 2–4-player space shootout; **Orbit Rally**, a cosmic score chase; and **Ribbon Run**, a trail arena. Play locally with a Game Player or a second person on the keyboard, or connect PureDesktop to PureServer to mix teammates and Game Players in the same live match. Grow a Garden, The Ninth Bell and Gourd Street have been retired. Their saved room data is retained, but they are no longer offered in the library or Active games.

## Play

Play is beneath the arena. When connected to PureServer, choose **Invite teammates**, select people by name (or search with `@name`), and send. Their desktop or PureCloud shows a persistent **Join** invitation; accepting opens the exact game and takes a seat. Invitations also remain in the conversation. Optional Game Players can share a match with humans.

**Active games** lists live matches, their players and agents, and waiting/playing/paused status. Join an open game or watch a round already underway. **Games** opens the library; **Back to game** returns without disconnecting. **Stop playing** leaves your game and opens the active list. The host can **End game for everyone**, or **Reset round** beside the arena to change players and restart. **Reconnect** preserves a seat during a transient disconnect; reopening with the same server account recovers its absent seat and round wins. The creator keeps the host controls while present. Disconnections pause play.

Codes remain an optional fallback under **Invite with a code instead** and **Enter code**. Everyone must use the same PureServer organisation. File and app access are checked when inviting and accepting; inviting does not grant access. Organisation-workspace games are visible to colleagues who can access that workspace, not private to invitees.

**Vector Wake:** Pilot the original Vela-9 through three hostile sectors. Fighters dive, cross the nose and climb to re-engage; the final sector introduces the Lattice Ark carrier. Fly with **arrow keys** or **WASD**, Q/E bank, Shift boosts and X brakes. **Z/C** performs an evasive roll left/right, costs 18 boost and has a 3.5-second cooldown; the first 0.65 seconds deflect incoming damage. Hold **Space** or the canvas to fire twin pulse cannons; watch their heat. Hold a target near the reticle for a seeker lock and press **R** to launch. **P** or Escape pauses. Mouse flight is optional, off by default, and supported by the Mouse button. Sound is opt-in. Touch flight buttons appear on smaller screens.

A ringed wireframe planet, a glowing carrier core, blue boost slipstreams, paired volleys, engine trails, perspective cannon barrels, bright laser cores, expanding wireframe shock rings and spinning hull fragments give the combat motion without external artwork. Incoming-fire warnings flag a predicted close approach, close calls earn points, and quick consecutive kills build a chain multiplier. Destroyed ships drop cyan shield cells: fly close to draw them in and restore 20 shield, 30 boost and earn 50 points. Cells expire after fourteen seconds. Cadet and Veteran differ in enemy cadence and damage. Shields regenerate after seven seconds without damage; sector clear restores shield and seeker ammunition. Victory requires all sixteen targets. Local play needs no server, agents or model calls. Canvas 2D projects original 3D geometry, so this game does not require WebGL; decorative trails, slipstreams and the rolling camera respect reduced-motion preferences. Invite up to three teammates through **Invite wingmates** in the flight briefing. Multiplayer uses PureServer; each pilot has independent controls, heat, ammunition, shields, target locks and a cockpit. Wingmate ships and coloured radar diamonds identify friends, with no friendly fire. The enemy count scales with the wing. Downed pilots return beside a survivor after eight seconds with brief protection; total wing loss ends the mission. Sector clears restore everyone, and victory and mission results are shared. The leader launches, resets and resumes; any pilot can pause. Disconnects pause the mission and the same account can reclaim its seat and progress. Join before launch; arrivals during a flight can watch and join the next briefing. Solo remains available offline.

Pitch has no stop: hold Up/W or Down/S to fly complete loops. Both pitch and yaw wrap continuously through full rotations. The tactical radar shows forward at the top, aft at the bottom, hostile triangles, wingmate diamonds, altitude stems and a box around the locked target. Click **Radar** to cycle 800m / 1.6km / 3.2km. Contacts beyond range stay marked at the rim. Radar range and visual/audio preferences are local to each cockpit.

**Letterlight:** A solo 5×5 letter board with six themed clues and a curated bonus vocabulary. Join neighbouring tiles horizontally, vertically or diagonally, using each tile once per word. Drag and release to submit; alternatively tap tiles or type a word and press Enter. Typed words show their connected path. Choose **Unhurried** or **Three minutes**. Play starts immediately, including when PureServer is connected, without a teammate or agent. The six theme words finish the board; Next board cycles through eight original themes. Words score 10 points per letter, plus 20 for a theme word and 100 for completing all six. Each of three hints reveals a clue’s first two letters and starting tiles; the hinted word earns 15 fewer points. Optional reveal ends the board without a completion bonus. Finished-board personal bests are stored on this device separately for each puzzle and mode. This is a curated word game, not a comprehensive dictionary checker. No extra libraries or external word services are used.

**Tether Crew:** Pilot small salvage tugs and recover five oversized loads together. Arrows (or WASD with one local human) thrust in any direction; movement has inertia. **Space** or **E** attaches a cable to the nearest cargo within reach, or releases it. Pull toward the recovery ring on the left and hold **Shift** to brake. Cargo must settle in the ring before it is secured. **P** pauses. A second local human uses WASD, F to hook and X to brake. Choose the close **Pilot view** with mini-map and off-screen cargo markers, or **Whole yard** for an overview. Optional synthesized game sounds can be enabled in the arena.

Loads have different masses. Two tugs can pull the same load; both pilots earn shared credit and a +25 teamwork bonus. Cargo condition affects value, and deliveries within 30 seconds build a chain bonus. Drifting debris bounces cargo and reduces its condition, while periodic magnetic tides push it sideways. **Four-minute shift** has a deadline; **Easy drift** lets you take your time. Game Players navigate, attach cables and tow physically, leaving a nearby unclaimed load for the human. All movement, cables, deliveries and scores use the existing PureServer session. Canvas art, physics and Web Audio tones are original code, with no new dependencies.

**Word Loom:** Solve eight clues together using a shared, shuffled letter rack. Type an answer and press Enter, or tap the tiles. Each tile may be used once per answer; the rack is reusable for every clue. Choose a three-minute round or relaxed play without a timer. Everyone can solve different clues at once and see where teammates are working. Correct answers earn 10 points plus two per letter, with two points deducted for each revealed hint letter. There are three shared hints per puzzle. Duplicate submissions score once. Game Player helps with unoccupied clues, giving people time to solve; it uses the authored answers locally. Completing all eight clues gives every teammate a completed puzzle. Play again advances through eight original themed puzzle sets, then cycles. These are curated clue puzzles, not an open dictionary word finder.

**Star Skirmish:** Left/Right turn in small increments, Up thrusts, Down brakes; hold **Space** to fire, **P** pauses. Three hits destroy a ship; it respawns after two seconds. Score one point per knockout; first to five, or highest score after 90 seconds, wins. Spawn shields last 1.8 seconds and end when you fire. Choose open space or asteroid cover. The second local pilot uses A/D to turn, W to thrust, S to brake and F to fire.

Orbit Rally uses the same gradual turning, thrust and brake controls. Ribbon Run keeps its four grid directions; Space pauses/resumes (the coordinator resumes a shared round). WASD controls only the second local human. Full screen is in the toolbar; **Games** returns to the selector. Arena controls remain beside the game. Both flight games interpolate ship rotation smoothly, cap turns to eight degrees per simulation step, and preserve quick taps between input samples. In Orbit Rally, collect cyan cores and bank them at moving gold gates: first to 20 points or the highest score after 90 seconds wins. Banking at least five cores earns a two-point bonus. Shift dashes with a brief shield; red hazards spill cargo. The second local pilot uses WASD and F to dash. Game Players use local navigation and hazard avoidance for Orbit Rally and a flood-fill strategy for Ribbon Run, not a remote language model or an assistant roster entry.

Live scoreboards stay inside all arenas, including full screen, and in the player panel. Tether Crew shows recovered cargo, a shared shift total and each pilot’s contribution. Word Loom shows the shared team total, discoveries and each person’s contributions. Star Skirmish shows knockouts and hull. Orbit Rally shows banked points and carried cores. Ribbon Run awards one point per step survived and 50 bonus points to the last survivor; eliminated players keep their points. Each new round resets points while round wins stay at the table. Scores are part of the shared simulation, so teammates see the same updates.

## Architecture

Game rules and rendering live here. `src/lib/shared.mjs` uses the platform's existing Yjs protocol. An elected peer coordinates the simulation at 10 ticks/second, accepts input and runs bots, and checkpoints JSON. All clients render the same state. Loss of a human connection or coordinator pauses play; transient connections retry. These are trusted collaborative games, not an adversarial competitive service with server-side anti-cheat.

Native sessions use `collab.remote` through the shell's authenticated main-process transport. PureServer credentials never enter the app. Browser sessions use the existing authenticated WebSocket. Older desktops without this transport retain local play. The shell work is in ps-suite PR #984. The retained legacy horror renderer uses the MIT-licensed Three.js dependency; the existing platform dependencies are React, Yjs, y-protocols and lib0 are already used by PureDesktop.

## Changes in 0.4.0

- Vector Wake cooperative flight for one to four human pilots using existing PureServer invitations and live sessions.
- Independent cockpits and controls, visible Vela wingmates, per-pilot equipment, kill credit, shared mission results and recovery beside a surviving teammate.
- Continuous pitch loops and smooth camera interpolation through angle wrapping.
- A shared buffered animation timeline for cockpits, enemy ships, wingmates, projectiles, targeting and radar. Uneven multiplayer updates no longer restart movement or snap enemy ships between ticks.
- Larger tactical radar with selectable range, altitude, forward/aft orientation, friend/foe shapes and locked-target highlighting.
- Existing solo checkpoints and other games remain supported. No new dependencies or model calls.

## Development and verification

`node scripts/preview-halloween.mjs /path/to/pure-server` opens a development-only, four-account browser fixture at `http://127.0.0.1:5666/qa-halloween.html`. It uses a temporary database and is excluded from the app bundle.

Place this repo at `apps/puregames` within a ps-suite checkout so the platform SDK file dependencies resolve. Run `npm test`, `npm run build`, or `npm run dev`. `python3 scripts/package.py` builds the installable compiled package (requires the shell's prebuilt-app installer).

`node scripts/preview-vector.mjs /path/to/pureserver` opens a disposable four-account cockpit fixture at `http://127.0.0.1:7197/qa-vector.html`. Open it twice and choose different pilots to test independent cockpits. These test accounts and the fixture are excluded from the production bundle.

`node scripts/check-vector-shared.mjs /path/to/pureserver [/path/to/ps-suite/packages/shell/src/modules/collab/remote.ts]` checks four authenticated clients, independent flight and firing, unrestricted pitch, shared pause, coordinator migration, account seat recovery and a complete three-sector cooperative mission. The optional shell path tests the native desktop adapter with structured-cloned frames; otherwise it uses authenticated browser WebSockets. It creates and removes a disposable server workspace and uses deterministic pilot inputs without model calls.

`node scripts/check-vector.mjs` flies both difficulty modes through all three sectors using ordinary controls and reports victory, score, accuracy and remaining hull/shields. It uses a deterministic simulated pilot, not a manual playthrough. Unit tests cover swept collisions, heat, locks, attack runs, barrel alignment, close calls, evasive rolls, pickup lifetime, pause/rematch and full cockpit rendering.

`node scripts/check-halloween.mjs /path/to/pure-server` checks four authenticated humans, independent movement and conversations, shared relics, pause, host migration and seat recovery against a disposable real PureServer workspace.

`node scripts/check-shared.mjs /path/to/pure-server [/path/to/ps-suite/packages/shell/src/modules/collab/remote.ts]` tests two authenticated clients, automatic seats, a mixed human/bot match, steering, dash, laser fire, knockout, banked and survival score replication, concurrent word submissions, shared hints, cooperative agent help, legacy noir checkpoint compatibility, Tether Crew’s two-human cable attachment and physical delivery with a Game Player, shared credit, shared pause and coordinator migration against a temporary server database/workspace. With the optional shell module it runs both clients through separate native transport adapters with IPC-style structured cloning, verifies reconnect without duplicate seats and shared round reset, and checks ticket ownership and path rejection. The combined test process can warn about two Yjs installations; production bundles have one.

App code and visual assets are original. Existing platform dependencies retain their own licences. No branded game names, artwork or external game code are bundled.
